## TS-01 Mobility architecture

**Hard requirements screened:** ground pressure ≤ 7 kPa (SR-MOB-01, S023), mobility retained after one drive failure (SR-MOB-06), delivery ≤ 1.5 t goal (MR-11). Each candidate's wheels were auto-sized to the same pressure and DP/W(40 %) ≥ 0.36 constraints at its own operational mass (`trades/mobility_trades.size_wheel_for`), so differences are architectural, not wheel-size artefacts.

**Findings.**
1. Slope capability is essentially identical across architectures (nominal ≈ 19.8°,
   conservative ≈ 14.7°): it is **traction-limited by the soil**, a ratio independent of gravity,
   so suspension sophistication does not buy gradeability.
2. Fully active six-leg and four-wheel active suspensions cost +80–100 kg over rocker-bogie for a larger obstacle
   capability (active-6) or fewer actuators (4-wheel) but **worse two-failure tolerance** for the four-wheeler
   (7.9° with two drives failed vs
   12.6°).
3. Passive rocker-bogie is lightest (1155 kg delivered). Adding body lowering + differential
   lock costs 30 kg.

**Decision: M2 six-wheel rocker-bogie with body lowering and differential lock.** Justification for the
30 kg over M1: (a) unloading any wheel for drive-ORU replacement
(self-serviceability, MR-13), (b) lowering the arm/crane bases 0.35 m for ground-level work (anchors, tow points),
(c) a sacrificial skid contact during winching. TS-04 shows the stability benefit alone is small — this is a
**descope candidate** recorded in the CDR.

**Rejected outright (qualitative):** tracks (metal tracks: dust ingress in many pin joints, mass; elastomer
tracks: vacuum/cold incompatible); legged walkers (ATHLETE-class: high power and actuator count for no
gradeability gain on a traction-limited surface).

## TS-02 Wheel architecture and sizing

Single-wheel sweep at operational mass 1250 kg (full grid in
`simulations/results/trade_wheel_sweep.csv`). Contact pressure self-regulates near 6–8 kPa (the soil pushes back as
the wheel sinks), so the 7 kPa rule effectively sets a minimum wheel width × diameter. The selected wheel
**Ø0.90 m × 0.40 m, 20 mm grousers** gives p = 6.64 kPa and sinkage
18 mm at the nominal wheel load 337 N.

| Wheel type | TRL | Heritage | Dust | Fatigue | Model confidence | Selected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rigid Ti-6Al-4V rim with grousers | 6 | VIPER/Yutu/Pragyan rigid wheels; MSL Al wheels | no mesh to fill; grousers shed regolith | rim cracking risk (MSL) mitigated by thicker Ti, low speed | high (rigid-wheel terramechanics applies) | **yes** |
| Woven wire-mesh (LRV-type) with Ti chevrons | 6 | Apollo LRV (S027, S029) | mesh openings admit dust; abrasion of wire crossings | low-cycle fatigue at wire crossings over 10-yr life and >1000 km unproven | low (flexible wheel) | no |
| Superelastic NiTi spring tyre | 4 | NASA GRC prototypes (no flight) | open spring mesh admits dust | superelastic fatigue promising but unqualified | low | no |
| Rigid Al 7075 wheel | 7 | MSL/M2020 wheels | as rigid Ti | MSL wheel punctures/cracks on sharp rocks (thin skin) | high | no |

**Calibration note.** The model with Lunar Sourcebook Table 9.14 soil over-predicts the measured Apollo LRV
traverse energy (k_cal = 0.62): either the in-situ soil is stronger than the trafficability set or flexible
wheels sink less. Energy estimates use k_cal; slope claims do **not** (they use the uncalibrated, conservative model).
