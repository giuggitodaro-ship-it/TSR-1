**Evaluation of the Todaro dual-asymmetric-arm hypothesis (directive §6A).** The heavy + dexterous pair (A3) is the
only architecture with full coverage, but costs 158 kg — 113 kg more than a
dexterous arm plus a cable-stayed crane boom (A4, coverage 0.94). In lunar gravity a heavy serial arm
carries its payload moment through every pitch joint (shoulder rating ≈ 1.5 kN·m for 150 kg at 2.3 m), while a
crane carries the load in tension/compression (LSMS principle, S015). **The heavy service arm is rejected.**

**Decision: A7 — two identical 7-DOF dexterous arms (20 kg rated, 1.6 m) + 150 kg crane boom.** Over A4 it adds
25 kg for (i) bimanual tasks without fixtures and (ii) removing the single most failure-prone
ORU as a single point of failure (λ = 0.08/yr → 55 % probability of an arm failure in 10 years; coverage after a
primary-arm failure 1.0 vs 0.0). The dual-arm idea therefore survives **in modified form** (identical dexterous arms,
crane instead of heavy arm). A4 is the recorded mass-descope fallback.

**Arm sizing basis:** torque at full horizontal extension in lunar gravity × dynamic factor 1.15 × motorisation factor
2 (L024), actuator mass from a torque-density MER (25 N·m/kg, ESTIMATE 15–40), Ti link tubes sized for 3 mm tip
deflection. Dexterous arm: 27.0 kg, shoulder rating 197 N·m.
Crane: 19.9 kg, reach 2.56 m.
